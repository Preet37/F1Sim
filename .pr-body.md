## The headline: most of "the graphics look cheap" is the tier, not the renderer

This is what the issue asked to settle, so it is measured rather than argued.

`probe:sharpness` gained a **grain metric** — mean absolute Laplacian of luma in six horizontal bands, read back inside the frame that drew it, at the resolution the scaler actually settled on — and a `SHARP_QUERY` passthrough, because every sharpness shot this project has ever taken was of `high` (a desktop detects `high`), and so described an image no phone has ever been shown.

Bahrain, cockpit, identical frame, scale 1.00, buffer ~2940×1396:

| configuration | band 1 (horizon) | band 2 (mid-distance) | band 5 (near field) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **`low` — what every phone got** | **20.3** | **63.6** | 3.0 |
| `low` + post chain, geometry still low | 2.1 | 18.7 | 6.7 |
| `medium` (post, full geometry, no MSAA) | 4.0 | 22.6 | 6.2 |
| `medium` + MSAA | 1.7 | 15.5 | 5.8 |
| **`high` — what the developing machine got** | 1.2 | 14.8 | 6.1 |

**The phone's image carried 16× the high-frequency speckle at the horizon and 4.3× in the middle distance, purely from the tier.** Chase view agrees within 10% on every row.

The isolation matters. Turning the post chain on and changing *nothing else* — geometry still low-detail, still no MSAA, still no shadows — recovers 9.7× of the horizon band and 3.4× of the mid-distance. **Geometry detail contributes nothing measurable** (low+post 18.7 against medium's 22.6 — slightly worse). MSAA is worth a further ~1.5×. This is the user's *"all of the other maps still have the weird black lines and grainy maps"*, and it is a tier artefact.

### Can a phone afford the post chain? Yes — and it is not even a trade.

Measured as **one** paired factor (`PERF_PAIR=tiertrade`), not composed from the others, because the composition is where the reasoning would go wrong: the chain's cost is nearly linear in pixels and the scene's is not.

| `medium` @ scale 0.50 vs `low` @ scale 1.00 | Bahrain | Monaco | Spa |
|---|---|---|---|
| medium @ 0.50 | 3.63ms | 5.17ms | 3.90ms |
| low @ 1.00 | 4.99ms | 4.99ms | 5.51ms |
| delta | **−1.35ms (27% cheaper)** | +0.30ms (6% dearer) | **−1.60ms (29% cheaper)** |
| per-cycle spread | −1.82..−1.18 | −1.15..+0.56 | −1.95..−1.25 |

And the same configuration on the grain metric: `medium` @ 0.50 measures **32.8** mid-distance against `low` @ 1.00's **63.6**, and **6.8** at the horizon against **20.3**.

**The post chain at a quarter of the pixels costs the same or less than no chain at full resolution, and is 1.9× / 3.0× cleaner. The phone was paying full price for the worse image.** That result is what sets the demotion ordering: `updateAutoTier` will not give up a tier until the resolution scaler is at its floor, and the justification is quoted in `AUTO_DEMOTE_MS`.

## What was built

- **`src/render/QualityTiers.ts`** — one place that decides what every `quality === 'high'` gate in `src/render/` means. There are about twenty across `CarMesh`, `TrackMesh`, `Signage`, `Rain`, `ParticleSystem`, `SkidMarks`, `SafetyCarMesh`, `CarStage`, `PostFX` and `Renderer`. Three tiers and four independent switches — post, shadows, MSAA, resolution ceiling — because the four do not scale together and a binary tier forced an all-or-nothing choice a phone always lost.
- **Detection cannot solve this and no longer pretends to.** `hardwareConcurrency` is clamped on iOS — every iPhone reports the same small number whatever silicon is behind it — and `deviceMemory` is not implemented in Safari at all. Any rule written on those reproduces the bug. So `auto` starts from a floor it is confident about and then **measures**: `Renderer.updateAutoTier` promotes after 8s under budget *while the resolution scaler is at its ceiling*, and demotes-and-latches once the scaler has run out of room. Same shape as the resolution scaler, which is the only thing in this renderer that has ever correctly described the machine it was on. **`auto` is still the default** and can no longer pin a phone at `low`.
- **`PostFX.enabled` was `quality === 'high'` and `readonly`.** The chain now builds and tears down on demand, so the setting takes effect without ending the session.
- **MSAA has two homes and they did not agree.** The GL context's `antialias` attribute antialiases when the chain is off and is *dead* when it is on — the samples that cost bandwidth then are the composer target's. Before three tiers existed the two could not disagree; `medium` is post-without-MSAA and would silently have paid for four samples a pixel. Both follow one switch, and the probe asserts both.
- **The shadow frustum is now configured unconditionally**, with only `castShadow` following the tier. It was configured *inside* the tier branch, which meant `sun.target` — moved onto the car every frame — **was not in the scene graph at all on a device that started low**, and shadows could never be turned on later without reproducing all of it.
- **Video tab**: tier, three switches, a resolution limit. Everything goes through `Renderer.setGraphics`, which returns what it could *not* apply, and the screen says so rather than claiming a change that has not happened. Post, shadows and resolution are live; MSAA needs a reload and the screen says so.
- **`main.ts` kept small**, per the contention warning: 8 hunks, four of which are one-token `menuQuality` swaps. The substance is the `video` tab.

## Note for #48 (near-field grain), as asked

I **did** touch the resolution scaler: its ceiling is now `min(MAX_SCALE, features.maxResolutionScale)` rather than `MAX_SCALE`, and a stated ceiling applies from the first frame. **Default behaviour is unchanged** — `auto` leaves the ceiling at 1.0 on every tier, deliberately, because pre-emptively softening a device that turns out to be fine is the mistake §6 is about. Confirmed: `probe:renderperf` still reports `scale 1.00->1.00, px 100%` on Bahrain, Monaco and Spa. I did **not** touch anisotropy or mip bias.

Two data points that bear on #48:

1. **The near field is the one band where grain gets slightly *worse* with the post chain on** (band 5: low 3.0 → medium 6.2 → high 6.1). That is consistent with the grade pass's twelve-tap AO, which `PostFX` already documents as "contributing around forty per cent of all the high-frequency variance on the road". **On `main` the reporting device is `low`, so it has no post chain and no AO — the speckle in their screenshot cannot be the AO.** It is in the scene render, which points at the texture-filtering candidates rather than at anything screen-space.
2. **This PR moves phones to `medium`, which turns that AO on.** Near-field grain on a phone may therefore get somewhat worse even as mid-distance grain improves 3–4×. That is a real, expected side effect and #48 should expect it.

The grain metric added here (`grainByBand` / `nearOverMid` in `sharp-out/*/manifest.json`, printed per shot) is the instrument for #48 and needs no further work to use.

## What I did NOT do

- **I did not measure on a phone.** There isn't one attached to this harness. `PERF_VIEWPORT` emulates the *geometry* a phone draws, not its GPU — an M5 is several times faster than any phone. Everything above is stated as ratios and per-factor deltas for that reason, and the adaptive `auto` pass exists precisely because I cannot know the answer for a device I do not have.
- **The machine was loaded for the entire measurement window** — load average 17–52 on ten cores, other agents running. Paired mode cancels *additive* drift; contention is *multiplicative*, so the desktop table (post at 4.3–5.1×) is inflated and PROJECT.md says so. The phone-geometry numbers have tight per-cycle spreads and are the ones I would defend. **Nothing here was measured at load under 8.**
- **I did not measure what shadows actually cost.** The existing `shadow` factor toggles `shadowMap.autoUpdate`, i.e. only the cascade re-render, which is 1–2%. The per-material sampling and the extra shader variant are isolated by no factor, so `high`'s definition rests on an unbacked assumption. Recorded in PROJECT.md §7 as an open item.
- **I did not widen the menu's tier.** `CarStage`, the intro, `TeamCreate` and `LiveryEditor` build a second GL context; `Renderer.menuQuality` deliberately keeps them at the pre-existing `high`-only behaviour so `probe:menucost` is not disturbed. Widening it is a separate measurement.
- **I did not re-tune the detail gates' contents** — `SkidMarks` capacity, `Signage` texture size, `CarMesh` segment counts, `Rain` drop count, `ParticleSystem` density. They now read `features.detail` (so `medium` gets the `high` values), but none of their numbers were touched.
- **I did not change any tolerance anywhere.**
- **`?quality=low|high` is unchanged for existing harnesses**; `medium`, `?post=`, `?shadows=`, `?msaa=` and `?res=` are added.
