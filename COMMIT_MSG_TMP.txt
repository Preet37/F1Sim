Wire the new ladder into the game, and drive it in a browser

Replaces CareerEngine with Career, which owns the three things a career mode gets
wrong when they are kept apart: the world (who exists), the season (what has
happened) and the player (where they are in it).

The hub, the standings, the grid handed to RaceEngine, both result paths and the
narrative events all move onto it, and two screens are new. The off-season is a
report rather than an alert box: it names the champion of every tier, who was
promoted and from where, who retired and who moved, all read from what the
off-season actually did. The end of a career is a season-by-season table instead
of a dead end.

The driven race no longer remaps the player to the id PLAYER before recording the
result. The engine already races them under their own id, and the remapping was a
source of drift between the driven path and the simulated one.

regress:career drives the real browser, because nothing else can: probe:season
runs a hundred career-years and probe:save round-trips a decade, and neither
touches a screen. It caught four things typecheck could not. The create screen
still promised promotion "on results, reputation opens the door", which stopped
being true. It offered a Formula 1 seat as the rookie's first drive. The hub read
"Formula 3 - Formula 3 - your car", because a spec series has no engine supplier
to name. And the name fields carried no input type, which is a mobile-keyboard
problem as much as a test one.

Three things the harness needed are worth recording for whoever writes the next
one: ?quality=low, or software GL pegs the main thread hard enough that Playwright
cannot resolve body; reducedMotion, or the entrance animations mean no element is
ever "stable"; and polling the dev server rather than matching its banner, which
is a race.
